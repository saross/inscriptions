# Claude-observations register — inscriptions project

## How to use this file

This is the **mirror** of `user-observations.md`. The defining axis is **who is observing whom**:

| Register | Observer | Subject | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`claude-observations.md`** (this file) | **Claude** | **Shawn** | "You chose the bounded, honest option" · "Marking the milestone is a good habit" |
| **`user-observations.md`** | **Shawn** | **Claude** | "That refactor you did really helped" |
| user-observations (the exception) | Claude *relaying* Shawn | Claude | Claude flags an in-the-moment "wow, that helped" from Shawn |
| grey zone — collaboration dynamics ("us") | either | us | "this handoff rhythm works / is a pitfall" — whoever raises it |

So: **claude-observations = Claude observing Shawn's working style, preferences-in-action, and
decision dynamics** — things worth carrying forward about *how Shawn works*, to calibrate future
instances. (`user-observations.md` is the reverse: Shawn's feedback on Claude's work.)

- Entries land at `/handoff` time as drafted candidates Shawn accepts/edits/discards.
- Format mirrors `user-observations.md`: `## claude-obs N — DATE: <title>` with **Pattern.** /
  **Lesson.** / **How to apply.** sub-blocks; cross-reference with `[[…]]`.
- Do not modify accepted entries in place — corrections land as new entries that cross-reference
  the older one.
- New CC instances **should read this file** (and `user-observations.md`) before substantial work.

> **Note (2026-06-20):** this register is new and **not yet fully wired up** — the `/handoff` and

## claude-obs 1 — 2026-06-20: Shawn adopts a new capability by understanding it, not just using it

**Pattern.** His first in-anger use of the Workflow (multi-agent orchestration) tool was deliberate
and structured: he opted in explicitly, asked me to *explain what I'd done* "to add workflows to my
routine repertoire," and confirmed the account/billing constraint (CC Max, well under limits) *before*
committing to the run. He treated the new tool as something to learn, not just to fire.

**Lesson.** Shawn adopts a new capability by **trying it on real work + acquiring the mental model +
verifying the cost/constraint envelope** — not ad hoc. He wants to come away able to wield the tool
himself next time.

**How to apply.** When introducing or using a new tool/capability, pair the action with a clear
explanation of *how it works* and flag the cost/constraint envelope up front. Expect him to want the
model, not just the result — a short "here's what this does and what it costs" is welcome, not noise.

## claude-obs 2 — 2026-06-20: Shawn consistently steers toward the bounded, honest option (anti-over-claim)

**Pattern.** Across the session he repeatedly chose scope discipline over reach: closing D13 with the
prereg-literal but *low-power* test rather than the fancier hierarchical B′ (and parking B′ once it
added nothing); deferring B′ and H6 baorista to the follow-up paper; and reframing the proposed
diachronic-population figure away from an absolute-population reconstruction toward an illustrative
*relative* trajectory — explicitly to avoid over-claiming. He also held a firm worked-example boundary
(method vignette in this paper; substantive history reserved for the companion).

**Lesson.** His default instinct is **keep scope tight, don't over-claim, defer cleanly.** He'd rather
report an honest null or a bounded result than stretch a claim past what the evidence carries.

**How to apply.** Default to proposing the bounded, honest version of an analysis/figure/claim, and
flag explicitly where a fancier option would over-reach; offer the stretch as clearly-optional or
deferred, not as the lead. Frame uncertainty plainly — he reads "indicative / illustrative / weak"
as features, not hedging.

## claude-obs 3 — 2026-06-20: Shawn marks milestones deliberately — acknowledging progress is part of the work

**Pattern.** On completing the entire analytical programme and its documentation, he explicitly asked
to "take a moment to celebrate finishing all the analyses and their documentation, this is a major
milestone" — pausing to name and mark the achievement rather than rolling straight into the next task
(figures).

**Lesson.** Shawn **values acknowledging progress and naming milestones**, not only grinding forward.
Marking a genuine inflection point is part of how he sustains a long project, not a digression from it.

**How to apply.** At genuine milestones (programme complete, lodgement, a hard problem cracked), *name
them and mark them* — a brief, specific acknowledgement of what was accomplished, grounded in the
actual achievement. Don't manufacture celebration for routine steps, but don't roll past a real one.
