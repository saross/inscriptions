## 1. Conference identity

**Full name:** Roman Archaeology Conference (RAC) / Theoretical Roman Archaeology
Conference (TRAC), 2026 joint edition.

- **RAC** = Roman Archaeology Conference. Premier international Roman
  archaeology conference, held biennially since 1995 (first at Reading).
  Organised by the Society for the Promotion of Roman Studies ("Roman
  Society"), alternating between Britain and continental Europe.
  ([Roman Archaeology Conference](https://www.romansocietyrac.ac.uk/))
- **TRAC** = Theoretical Roman Archaeology Conference. Annual since 1991
  (first at Newcastle, organised by Eleanor Scott). Created to bring
  theory-driven critique — inspired by TAG (Theoretical Archaeology Group) —
  to a sub-discipline its founders judged under-theorised. Originally a
  one-off; turned annual due to demand. 31 conferences held by 2023.
  ([TRAC About](https://www.trac.org.uk/about/);
  [Introduction: TRAC 1991](https://traj.openlibhums.org/article/id/3651/))
- **Joint format:** Every other year RAC and TRAC are co-located as a single
  RAC/TRAC event. 2026 is a joint year.
  ([Roman Society — RAC overview](https://www.romansocietyrac.ac.uk/))

**Theme (2026 edition):** "Roman Archaeology Beyond the Fringes of the Empire."
Key thematic threads identified by the hosts: urbanism, networks (economic
and cultural exchange), archive archaeology, and theoretical frameworks for
decolonising the discipline.
([UrbNet — Beyond the Fringes](https://urbnet.au.dk/news/nyhed/artikel/ractrac-2026-aarhus))

**Organisers (host institution):** Aarhus University.

- Lead: Professor Rubina Raja (Chair of Classical Archaeology and Art;
  Director of the Centre for Urban Network Evolutions, UrbNet).
- Co-organisers: Dr Olympia Bobou, Dr Ilaria Bucci, Dr Vinnie Nørskov.
- Conference email: ractrac2026@gmail.com; primary contact Vinnie Nørskov
  (klavn@cas.au.dk).
  ([conferences.au.dk landing page](https://conferences.au.dk/ractrac-2026);
  [UrbNet save-the-date](https://urbnet.au.dk/news/nyhed/artikel/ractrac-2026-aarhus-save-the-date))

### Session abstract (verbatim, abridged)

> "Roman epigraphy has traditionally focused on qualitative analysis of
> individual inscriptions… This session explores how quantitative
> approaches to epigraphic evidence can transform our understanding of
> Roman society and culture. By applying computational methods, statistical
> analysis, and data visualisation techniques to large epigraphic corpora,
> we can address inherent biases in the surviving record, quantify
> uncertainty in our interpretations, and restore missing contextual
> information. These distant reading approaches enable us to uncover
> patterns undetectable by traditional methods, revealing demographic
> shifts, tracing cultural diffusion, and identifying evolving linguistic
> conventions… The session aims to demonstrate how quantitative methods
> can complement, rather than replace, traditional epigraphic scholarship…
> This session invites archaeologists, historians, digital humanists, and
> computational linguists to engage in interdisciplinary dialogue about
> the future of epigraphic studies and its potential to reshape our
> understanding of Roman society through the lens of big data analysis."

The session explicitly welcomes "statistical methods for mitigating
selection biases in the epigraphic record" and "digital methodologies for
integrating epigraphic data with spatial and archaeological longitudinal
data" — i.e., Bayesian / SPA-style approaches fit squarely in scope.
([Session Abstracts PDF, TRAC7](https://romansocietyrac.ac.uk/live/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/RAC_TRAC-2026-Session-Abstracts.pdf))

### Adela's paper abstract (verbatim from Book of Abstracts)

The full abstract is reproduced in the official Book of Abstracts. Key
points (verbatim and paraphrased):

- Frames the project against Hopkins (1987), Frier (1982), Saller and Shaw
  (1984/1987), Shaw (1987, 2002), Lelis–Percy–Verstraete (2003), Scheidel
  (2007 *Debating Demography*).
- "I applied Shaw and Saller's methodology for assessing women's age at
  marriage to the EDH people and LIST datasets."
- "Of ~90,000 inscriptions with identifiable persons, only ~6,000 contained
  persons whose gender, role, age at death, and family relationship was
  defined enough for analysis."
- Finding: "dedications to wives overtake dedications to daughters around
  age 19–20. The marriage age shifts down in larger urban contexts and up
  in rural regions."
- Caveat acknowledged: anomalous conjugal dedications to girls aged 5–15
  trace back to mis-encoded ages (e.g., "vixit cum viro novem continuis
  annis" miscoded as age 5 at death) — "detailed age review and curation
  will be necessary before arriving at any firm conclusions."
- Closing motivation: addressing Scheidel's question of whether marriage
  ages "could change considerably over time, from one century to the
  next, in response to the disease environment and economic
  opportunities" (2007, 401).

([Book of Abstracts PDF](https://conferences.au.dk/fileadmin/conferences/2026/rac-trac-2026/RAC_TRAC_BoA.pdf))

### Shawn's paper abstract (verbatim from Book of Abstracts)

- Frames SPA against archaeological "dates-as-data" tradition (Williams
  2012). Applies SPA to **LIRE** (>180,000 Roman-era inscriptions).
- "Only one paper, however, appears to have considered using SPA on
  inscriptions (Hanson 2021)."
- Demonstrations at province and municipality level in the western empire.
- Interpretation: SPA output read **not as raw population proxy** but as a
  proxy for "complexity (the sum of various components, including
  population, wealth, social stratification, and other factors)."
- Disentangling step: comparison with Hanson and Ortman population
  estimates to "isolate the population component and begin to understand
  the information conveyed by this composite measure."

([Book of Abstracts PDF](https://conferences.au.dk/fileadmin/conferences/2026/rac-trac-2026/RAC_TRAC_BoA.pdf))

## 4. Audience profile and theoretical orientation

**Combined audience.** RAC pulls a broader and more traditional Roman
archaeology crowd (academics, museum and field professionals, postgrads);
TRAC has historically attracted early-career researchers and postgraduates
oriented to critical theory. The joint years draw both.
([Roman Society — RAC overview](https://www.romansocietyrac.ac.uk/);
[TRAC About](https://www.trac.org.uk/about/))

**Scale.** Participatory model: "more than half the delegates are speaking,
and it is customary that all delegates to RAC/TRAC pay the conference
fee."
([Booking page](https://www.romansocietyrac.ac.uk/ractrac-2026/booking/))
No headline attendance figure for 2026 published; 2024 (UCL London) ran
**~40 sessions**. 2026 schedule shows **9 parallel session tracks** running
across Thursday PM, Friday all day, and Saturday all day — implying
several hundred speakers and likely 400–600 total delegates as an order-
of-magnitude estimate. [NEEDS VERIFICATION — Section 8.]

**Disciplinary mix.** Roman archaeologists (urbanism, ceramics, frontiers,
necropoleis); ancient historians (demography, epigraphy); digital
humanists and computational classicists; small numbers of classicists,
epigraphers, art historians. The TRAC7 session explicitly invites
"archaeologists, historians, digital humanists, and computational
linguists."

**Theoretical orientation (TRAC heritage).**

- Critical, theory-aware: identity, agency, decolonisation, post-
  colonial / post-processual readings.
- 2024 London edition foregrounded "new scientific applications,
  decentering and decolonizing Roman archaeology, globalization and
  materiality, and archaeological ethics."
  ([RAC/TRAC 2024 timetable](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/social-historical-sciences/archaeology/news-and-events/conferences/ractrac-conference-2024-timetable))
- 2026 has explicit decolonisation plenary: **TRAC Roundtable — "Decolonising
  the Roman past"** (Saturday closing) and a parallel **RAC Roundtable —
  "Whither theory in Roman studies?"** (Friday late afternoon, Aulaen).
  ([Programme/Rooms PDF](https://conferences.au.dk/fileadmin/conferences/2026/rac-trac-2026/RACTRAC2026_Programme_Rooms_FINAL.pdf))
- Strong appetite for computational, quantitative, and open-science work
  within the theoretical tent — visible in TRAC7 (quantitative epigraphy)
  and TRAC8 ("Graphs have feelings too: empirical approaches to
  theoretical problems"). The community has reconciled to "big data" as
  legitimate theoretical practice if framed reflexively.

**Pitching implication.** A Bayesian-statistics + open-science talk
won't be the most exotic thing in the room — TRAC7 is curated for
exactly this audience. The audience will reward:

- Honest treatment of corpus bias (Adela's miscoded-age anomalies note
  is on point).
- Explicit linkage of method to historical questions (rather than
  method-for-method's-sake).
- Reflexivity about what the numbers *can* and *cannot* license.
- Engagement with the post-colonial / decolonising current — for SPA
  applied to "the western empire," some thought to how the metric
  behaves at the periphery / non-urban margins anticipates the most
  obvious critical question.

## 5. Format expectations (talk length, Q&A, session structure)

- **Talk slot:** 20 minutes per paper (standard RAC/TRAC slot). Call for
  Papers states "each up to 20 minutes long."
  ([Call for Papers](https://www.romansocietyrac.ac.uk/ractrac-2026/call-for-papers/))
- **Discussion:** Batched at the end of each half-session, not per-paper.
  TRAC7's Friday afternoon block runs five 20-minute slots back-to-back
  with a single **"Discussion"** entry at **15:20** (final 10 minutes of
  the block). Friday morning block has no explicit per-block discussion
  slot — discussion presumably folds into the lunch break / coffee.
  ([Session Programme PDF](https://conferences.au.dk/fileadmin/conferences/2026/rac-trac-2026/Session-Programme-RACTRAC2026.pdf))
- **Talk length implication for "12-minute" target.** A 20-minute slot
  with batched discussion typically means a 15–18 minute talk leaving
  buffer for changeover. A **12-minute talk leaves room for one or two
  in-line questions or just clean changeover** — conservative and safe;
  Adela will not be cut off and may have room for a single immediate
  clarification before the next speaker. Confirm with session organisers
  whether per-talk Q&A is desired or whether discussion is purely
  batched.
- **Slide expectations:** Not specified in any RAC/TRAC 2026 documentation
  I located. RAC/TRAC norm is single-projector, 16:9, MS PowerPoint /
  PDF; presenter laptop or USB stick to a session-room machine. [NEEDS
  VERIFICATION — Section 8.]
- **Chair:** Session organisers (Heřmánková, Glomb, Kaše) presumably
  chair their own session; the programme PDF does not explicitly assign
  chairs per block. [NEEDS VERIFICATION.]
- **Plenaries.** Two plenary events relevant to the TRAC7 community:
  - Thursday 17:45–18:45 (Aulaen): **Keynote — "Roman archaeology in the
    Mediterranean core and frontier zones."**
  - Friday 16:00–17:30 (Aulaen): **RAC Roundtable — "Whither theory in
    Roman studies?"** (immediately after TRAC7 ends).
  - Saturday 17:00–18:30 (Aulaen): **TRAC Roundtable — "Decolonising the
    Roman past"**, followed by closing ceremony and awards (18:30–19:30).
  ([Programme/Rooms PDF](https://conferences.au.dk/fileadmin/conferences/2026/rac-trac-2026/RACTRAC2026_Programme_Rooms_FINAL.pdf))
- **Social events.**
  - Thursday 18:45–19:45: Wine reception (Vandrehallen & Antikmuseet).
  - Friday 20:00–01:00: **TRAC Party** (Studenterhus Café). Useful for
    informal continuation of TRAC7 conversations.
