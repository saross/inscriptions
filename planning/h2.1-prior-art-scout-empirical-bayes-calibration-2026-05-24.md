# Prior-art report: empirical-Bayes calibration cohort for epigraphic mixture models

## Executive summary

This is a **partially-solved problem in adjacent fields, but almost certainly a novel application in epigraphy**. The specific combination — using a narrow-dated subset of a corpus as an empirical-Bayes calibration cohort to constrain the `p_gen` component in a mixture model separating editorial-convention artefacts from genuine ancient temporal signal — does not appear to have been attempted for epigraphic data. The two best-developed adjacent traditions are: (1) palaeoclimate transfer-function methods (BUMPER, SCUBIDO), which use a modern calibration period of well-observed data to learn the proxy–climate relationship before projecting onto fossil data, and (2) Bayesian OSL mixture models (Christophe et al. 2018), which decompose an observed dose distribution into a "well-bleached" genuine component and a residual component, with informative priors possible on either. The theoretical framework for using informative priors on one mixture component to break a likelihood ridge is well-established (Wraith et al. 2014; Betancourt 2017; Semochkina & Walsh 2025), and the primary failure mode — calibration-cohort bias propagating into corpus-wide estimates when the calibration subset is systematically unrepresentative — is documented in palaeodemography and psychometric modelling (Spektor & Kellen 2018). The consultation with Martin Drechsler is well-timed: the structural analogy to Bayesian source-apportionment problems, where well-characterised reference sources are used to anchor identifiability, is the strongest prior art for the proposed fix.

## Recommendations

**Use directly (adopt the conceptual design without modification):**

- **SCUBIDO approach** (row 1) is the best existence proof for the two-stage "calibration subset → Bayesian model → main corpus" architecture. The Boyall et al. 2025 paper will be credible to Martin Drechsler as a close analogue from earth sciences. The difference is that SCUBIDO uses the calibration period parameters as fixed values in the projection phase (not as hyperpriors), whereas the H2.1 proposal would use the narrow-dated SPA as a prior distribution — a slightly more flexible and defensible approach.

- **Semochkina & Walsh 2025** (row 2) is the tutorial-level reference to bring to the consultation. It provides the vocabulary ("resolving non-identifiability via informative priors from additional evidence") and demonstration of conditions under which this succeeds or fails. Use it to frame the structural fix methodologically.

**Adapt approach:**

- **Christophe et al. 2018 MD2** (row 3) should inform the implementation. The mixture of a parametric (well-characterised) component and a non-parametric (flexible) component — where the parametric component's prior can be seeded from a reference subset — is the most direct structural analogue for `p_conv + p_gen`. The JAGS implementation is inspectable and could be adapted.

- **Wraith et al. 2014** (row 4) demonstrates the mechanism by which informative priors stabilise Gaussian mixture components over a time axis. Useful for justifying the approach to a statistician, and for choosing the prior structure (informative on the mean trajectory, weakly informative on the variance, etc.).

- **Betancourt 2017** (row 5) is the authoritative Stan reference for *why* the proposed fix should work: strong non-exchangeable prior information on one component breaks the likelihood ridge that makes the other component (and the mixing weight α) unidentifiable. Present to Martin as the mechanism.

- **Price et al. 2021** (row 8) provides the identifiability analysis vocabulary for the mixture SPD problem specifically. Their linear-algebra identifiability test is adaptable to the H2.1 model.

**Read primarily as critique material:**

- **Spektor & Kellen 2018** (row 6): the most important caveat. If the narrow-dated subset's SPA is a noisy or biased signal (which it is — dominated by military/imperial/dedicatory categories), empirical priors derived from it may move the posterior without genuinely resolving the ridge. This is the failure mode to present proactively to Martin.

- **Crema 2022 review** (row 10): documents the full catalogue of bias sources in temporal frequency models. Every bias listed (taphonomy, research interest, differential survival) applies to the epigraphic case and must be addressed when characterising how representative the narrow-dated subset is.

**Ignore (not a match):**

- **Ithaca / Assael et al.** (row 16): discriminative deep learning model; no extractable generative prior on the temporal distribution. Architecturally incompatible.

- **Glomb et al. 2022** (row 13): uses Monte Carlo temporal modelling but treats the general corpus as a normalisation baseline rather than an informative prior. Weaker version of the idea, no mixture-model component separation.

## Build-vs-adopt verdict

**Build from scratch, informed by the approaches in rows 1–5.**

There is no existing package or implementation that combines all three elements: (a) a corpus-wide Gaussian-random-walk mixture model, (b) a narrow-dated calibration cohort used as an informative hyperprior on the genuine component, and (c) application to epigraphic SPA data. The components exist separately:

- The GRW non-parametric component is in `baorista` (row 7).
- The two-stage calibration-cohort-to-prior architecture is in SCUBIDO and BUMPER (rows 1, 12).
- The theory for using informative priors on one component to break a likelihood ridge is in Betancourt 2017 and Wraith et al. 2014 (rows 4, 5).
- The tutorial on resolving non-identifiability by informative priors from additional evidence is in Semochkina & Walsh 2025 (row 2).

The practical recommendation for the consultation is therefore: **build the calibration-cohort extension to the existing `02-mixture-fit.py` model, using Semochkina & Walsh (2025) as the methodological framing, SCUBIDO as the existence proof, Christophe et al. (2018) as the closest structural analogue for the mixture decomposition, and Spektor & Kellen (2018) as the honest accounting of failure conditions**. None of these require licence acquisition. The specific implementation question — whether the narrow-dated SPA should enter as (i) a Gaussian-smoothed fixed prior mean on `p_gen`, (ii) a hyperprior on the GRW smoothing parameter, or (iii) a likelihood term from the narrow-dated sub-corpus jointly — is the key question to pose to Martin.
