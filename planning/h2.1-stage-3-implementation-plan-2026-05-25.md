# H2.1 Stage 3 implementation plan — modified mixture model with empirical-Bayes calibration cohort

## 1. Executive summary

Stage 3 replaces three pieces of the current H2.1 mixture model — the
learned `tier_weights @ tier_basis` convention component, the zero-mean
Gaussian-random-walk prior on the genuine component, and the centred
parameterisation of that random walk — with their empirically-anchored
and computationally-improved equivalents, while leaving the α prior
and the sampler defaults essentially unchanged. The convention
component becomes a fixed (or near-fixed) vector derived from 119,142
F1+F3 inscriptions (Stage 1); the genuine component is given an
informative prior centred on the type-reweighted Cohort B log-density
shape (Stage 2), with per-bin strength from a bootstrap-derived
sigma_prior; a Gaussian-random-walk smoothness layer survives but is
written in the non-centred form already validated by F3, giving a
45-50× effective-sample-size improvement at zero posterior-shape cost.
The α prior shifts from Beta(2, 2) to Beta(1, 1) by default, so that
α is no longer mechanically pulled toward 0.5 — small move on the
evidence, but it eliminates a known prior-pull contribution to the
recovery bias. The intended effect is to break the likelihood ridge
that pulled posterior α toward the middle of its range in the
2026-05-22 recovery-grid FAIL (mean bias ±0.04 to ±0.07 across the
α grid). The remaining risks are: (i) the calibration cohort is
itself a non-random subsample of LIRE and may export its own bias
into the prior (Spektor & Kellen 2018); (ii) the empirical p_conv
basis is corpus-wide, so any province- or type-level convention
heterogeneity is not yet absorbed; and (iii) Stage 4's recovery grid
is run on synthetic data generated from the *old* basis, so a clean
pass requires us to also re-run the synthetic generator with the new
basis or accept that the diagnostic is a stress test on a slightly
mis-specified data-generating process.

## 2. Current model architecture

The current production model
(`runs/2026-05-22-recovery-grid-validation/code/02-cell-mixture-fit.py`)
implements the preregistered specification at prereg §3 lines 165-210.
The diagram below uses `~` for "is distributed as", `=` for
deterministic assignment, and `@` for matrix multiplication.

```
                     Priors                                Likelihood
                     ------                                ----------

    α ~ Beta(2, 2)                           ─┐
    tier_weights ~ Dirichlet([1, 1, 1])      ─┤
    σ_smooth   ~ HalfNormal(1)               ─┤
    log_pgen_increments[t]                    │
        ~ Normal(0, σ_smooth)  for t=1..79   ─┤
                                              │
                                              ▼
    p_conv = tier_weights @ tier_basis  ←── tier_basis (3 × 80)
                                              │           (data input,
                                              │            built from
                                              │            template_intervals_by_tier
                                              │            in design.json)
                                              │
    log_pgen_raw = concat([0], cumsum(log_pgen_increments))
    p_gen        = softmax(log_pgen_raw)
                                              │
                                              ▼
    p_mix = α · p_conv + (1 − α) · p_gen
                                              │
                                              ▼
    y_obs ~ Multinomial(N, p_mix)   ←── observed 80-bin counts
```

Components that change in the modified version:

- `tier_weights ~ Dirichlet([1, 1, 1])` — replaced by either a fixed
  empirical weight vector or a tighter Dirichlet anchored on the
  Stage 1 slab-type weights (Martin decision).
- `tier_basis` (3-tier, 80 bins) — replaced by a 9-row empirical
  slab-type basis (or by the precomputed 80-vector `p_conv_empirical`
  if the weights are fixed and we collapse the matrix multiplication
  at ingestion).
- `log_pgen_increments[t] ~ Normal(0, σ_smooth)` — augmented with an
  empirical centring term anchored on the Stage 2 reweighted log-density
  shape, with per-bin (or scalar) strength from `sigma_prior`.
- The increment block is **rewritten as a non-centred reparameterisation**
  (per F3, mathematically identical, 45-50× ESS).
- `α ~ Beta(2, 2)` — proposed change to `Beta(1, 1)` (Martin decision).

The likelihood (Multinomial) and the sampler stack (default PyMC NUTS,
no numpyro per the preregistration gate) do not change.

## 3. Modified model architecture

The modified model swaps in the two empirical building blocks, marked
below as data inputs distinguished from the model parameters.

```
       Stage 1 outputs              Stage 2 outputs                 Model parameters
       ---------------              ---------------                 -----------------

       p_conv_empirical (80)      pgen_prior_log_mean (80)         α ~ Beta(1, 1)       ←── (Martin: 1,1 vs 2,2)
       slab_weights (9)           pgen_prior_log_sigma (80)        σ_smooth ~ HalfNormal(1)
       slab_basis (9 × 80)        sigma_prior_scalar (≈ 0.044)     z_pgen[t] ~ Normal(0, 1)
                                                                    (raw non-centred increments,
                                                                     t = 1..79)
                │                          │                                │
                ▼                          ▼                                ▼

       p_conv = p_conv_empirical                                    log_pgen_centring[t]
                                                                       = pgen_prior_log_mean[t]
       ── OR ──                                                              ← data
                                                                    log_pgen_increments[t]
       slab_w ~ Dirichlet(η · slab_weights)        (Martin choice)     = σ_smooth · z_pgen[t]
       p_conv = slab_w @ slab_basis                                                          (non-centred GRW; F3)

                                                                    log_pgen_raw
                                                                       = concat([0], cumsum(log_pgen_increments))

                                                                    log_pgen_anchored
                                                                       = pgen_prior_log_mean
                                                                         + sigma_prior · (log_pgen_raw − mean(log_pgen_raw))

                                                                    p_gen = softmax(log_pgen_anchored)

                                                              ▼
                       p_mix = α · p_conv + (1 − α) · p_gen
                                                              ▼
                       y_obs ~ Multinomial(N, p_mix)   ←── observed 80-bin counts
```

Diagram conventions: solid arrows are deterministic transformations;
`~` lines name stochastic priors. Boxes on the left and middle (data
inputs from Stage 1 and 2 CSVs) are *fixed* inputs to the model
graph, not parameters being inferred. The right-hand column lists the
*remaining* free parameters: `α`, `σ_smooth`, the 79 raw non-centred
increments `z_pgen`, and optionally the 9-element `slab_w`
(Dirichlet-prior variant only).

Three substantive structural shifts to notice. First, p_conv enters
the model as a constant (or near-constant) vector, not as a learned
linear combination. This is the SCUBIDO move (Boyall et al. 2025):
the well-characterised component is fixed from reference data, not
estimated alongside the unknown component. Second, log p_gen is no
longer centred on zero but on the Stage 2 reweighted log-density
shape — the BUMPER (Holden et al. 2017) move, with the cohort prior
acting as a centring distribution rather than as a likelihood term.
Third, the GRW survives in non-centred form, so the inference engine
explores a near-spherical geometry rather than the funnel that made
the centred version converge slowly. The α prior is unencumbered by
prior centring on 0.5 (Beta(1, 1) is the uniform on the unit
interval), so α can move freely under the data and the new prior on
p_gen.

## 4. Component-by-component change spec

### 4.1 p_conv (convention component)

- **Current implementation.** A learnable 80-vector built as
  `tier_weights @ tier_basis`, with `tier_weights ~ Dirichlet([1, 1, 1])`
  and `tier_basis` a 3 × 80 matrix derived from the
  `template_intervals_by_tier` block of `design.json`.
- **Proposed implementation.** Either (a) a fixed 80-vector loaded
  from `runs/2026-05-24-empirical-pconv/outputs/tables/empirical-pconv-vector.csv`
  and inserted as a `pm.Data`/`pm.ConstantData` node; or (b) a near-fixed
  vector recovered as `slab_w @ slab_basis` where `slab_w` is given a
  tight Dirichlet prior centred on the empirical slab weights.
- **Mathematical statement.** Variant (a): `p_conv = p_conv_empirical`.
  Variant (b): `slab_w ~ Dirichlet(η · w_empirical)`,
  `p_conv = slab_w @ slab_basis`, where `w_empirical` is the 9-vector
  of empirical slab weights and `η` is a concentration hyperparameter
  (e.g., η = 200 gives a posterior contraction comparable to a sample of
  ~ 200 inscriptions; η = 119142 reproduces the empirical weights
  almost exactly).
- **Rationale.** The Stage 1 empirical basis differs from the closest
  current placeholder (`pilot_proxy`) by L1 = 0.31 — ~ 15 % of total
  mass mis-allocated. Fixing p_conv at the empirical value removes
  this mis-specification and removes the ridge dimension along which
  α and tier_weights traded mass. The Dirichlet-prior variant
  preserves a small amount of flexibility while still concentrating
  on the empirical shape.
- **Design decision to flag for Martin.** Fixed vs Dirichlet-prior.
  Fixed is the SCUBIDO design philosophy and the simpler model;
  Dirichlet-prior is the Wraith et al. 2014 design philosophy and
  preserves a hedge against corpus-level error in the empirical
  weights. Recommended default: fixed; flag for confirmation.

### 4.2 p_gen prior

- **Current implementation.** Zero-mean Gaussian random walk on
  log-density increments, with `log_pgen_increments[t] ~ Normal(0, σ_smooth)`
  and `σ_smooth ~ HalfNormal(1)`. After cumsum and softmax this gives
  a smoothness-only prior — flat on the linear scale in expectation.
- **Proposed implementation.** An informative prior on log p_gen
  centred on the Stage 2 reweighted log-density shape, with per-bin
  strength from `sigma_prior`. The GRW smoothness layer is preserved
  on top of the centring, so the model still penalises sharp
  bin-to-bin transitions; the non-centred reparameterisation from F3
  is used unconditionally.
- **Mathematical statement.** For bins t = 1..80,
  `log_pgen[t] ~ Normal(μ_t, σ_prior_t)` where `μ_t` =
  `pgen_prior_log_mean[t]` and `σ_prior_t` = `pgen_prior_log_sigma[t]`
  (or a scalar `σ_prior_scalar` ≈ 0.044, the median across well-covered
  bins). Smoothness is enforced by the layer
  `log_pgen[t] − log_pgen[t−1] ~ Normal(0, σ_smooth)`,
  `σ_smooth ~ HalfNormal(1)`, implemented as a non-centred increment
  block.
- **Rationale.** Centring p_gen on a known shape from outside the
  fitted likelihood is the canonical mechanism for breaking a
  mixture-model identifiability ridge (Betancourt 2017; Semochkina &
  Walsh 2025). It is exactly the SCUBIDO/BUMPER architecture, and
  the closest structural analogue is Christophe et al. 2018, who
  separate the well-bleached (signal) from poorly-bleached (artefact)
  components of an OSL dose distribution using informative priors on
  the signal side.
- **Design decision to flag for Martin.** (i) Per-bin
  `σ_prior_t` (full Stage 2 output) vs scalar `σ_prior_scalar`
  ≈ 0.044. Per-bin is what BUMPER does and naturally widens the
  prior in thin-coverage edge bins; scalar is simpler and avoids any
  spurious widening from bootstrap noise at the edges. Recommended
  default: per-bin. (ii) Whether to inflate the empirical
  `σ_prior` by a robustness factor (e.g., 1.5× or 2×) to acknowledge
  that the calibration cohort is biased and the bootstrap CI is a
  lower bound on prior uncertainty (Stage 2 §9). Recommended default:
  inflate by 1.5× as a conservative hedge; flag for Martin.

### 4.3 α prior

- **Current implementation.** `α ~ Beta(2, 2)`, a symmetric prior
  with mode 0.5 and ~ 5 % mass above 0.95.
- **Proposed implementation.** `α ~ Beta(1, 1)`, equivalent to
  `Uniform(0, 1)`.
- **Mathematical statement.** `α ~ Beta(1, 1)`.
- **Rationale.** F1 showed that the Beta(2, 2) → Uniform(0, 1) move
  shifts the α posterior by only +0.025 on average at α=0.95 — the
  prior is not the dominant source of bias. But the move is free,
  and once p_gen is informatively centred (4.2), the α prior should
  not be carrying any independent mass-centring work. Beta(1, 1)
  leaves α unencumbered by prior pull.
- **Design decision to flag for Martin.** Beta(1, 1) vs Beta(2, 2)
  vs something weakly informative (Beta(0.5, 0.5), Jeffreys' prior;
  or Beta(1, 4) if we believe a priori that the corpus is signal-
  dominated). Recommended default: Beta(1, 1); flag for Martin in
  case he wants a stronger informative justification.

### 4.4 GRW parameterisation

- **Current implementation.** Centred: `log_pgen_increments[t] ~
  Normal(0, σ_smooth)` with `σ_smooth ~ HalfNormal(1)`.
- **Proposed implementation.** Non-centred:
  `z_pgen[t] ~ Normal(0, 1)`, then
  `log_pgen_increments[t] = σ_smooth · z_pgen[t]` as a Deterministic
  node, with `σ_smooth ~ HalfNormal(1)` unchanged.
- **Mathematical statement.** `z_pgen[t] ~ Normal(0, 1)`;
  `log_pgen_increments[t] = σ_smooth · z_pgen[t]`. Marginal prior on
  `log_pgen_increments` is `Normal(0, σ_smooth)` — identical to the
  centred form.
- **Rationale.** F3 verified mathematical equivalence via 1 000-draw
  prior-equivalence check (variance ratio 0.946, all gates passed)
  and demonstrated 45-50× min-ESS-bulk improvement, R-hat falling
  from ~ 1.04 to ~ 1.0008, and divergences eliminated. Adopted
  unconditionally for the modified model.
- **Design decision to flag for Martin.** None — already validated.
  Noted in the Stage 3 plan for completeness only.

### 4.5 Sampler

- **Current implementation.** Default PyMC NUTS, `pm.sample(...)`
  with no specific backend selection. CLI default: 2000 draws, 1000
  tune, 4 chains, target_accept = 0.95, cores = 1 (within-fit;
  orchestrator parallelises across cells).
- **Proposed implementation.** Same. The preregistration gate (§3
  line 209) excludes `numpyro` as an alternative backend before
  registered amendment; we honour that.
- **Mathematical statement.** None (sampler implementation, not
  model).
- **Rationale.** With non-centred GRW the default sampler is already
  comfortably mixing (ESS in the thousands, R-hat ≤ 1.001). No need
  to change.
- **Design decision to flag for Martin.** None.

### 4.6 Init

- **Current implementation.** Default PyMC init strategy (`init="auto"`,
  which under PyMC 5.x uses `jitter+adapt_diag`).
- **Proposed implementation.** Same. With the informative prior in
  place the typical starting point is much closer to the high-mass
  region of the posterior, so init quality matters less than under
  the current model.
- **Mathematical statement.** None (sampler setup).
- **Rationale.** No evidence init is a bottleneck.
- **Design decision to flag for Martin.** None.

        "pgen_prior_log_mean": sigma["log_p_gen_mean"].to_numpy(),
        "pgen_prior_log_sigma": sigma_per_bin,
        "sigma_prior_scalar": float(np.median(sigma_per_bin)),

### 5.2 Modified model construction

```python
# === ILLUSTRATIVE — not working code ===
# runs/2026-XX-stage-3-empirical-bayes/code/02-mixture-fit-empirical-bayes.py
import numpy as np
import pymc as pm
import pytensor.tensor as pt


def build_model_empirical_bayes(
    y: np.ndarray,
    p_conv_empirical: np.ndarray,
    pgen_prior_log_mean: np.ndarray,
    pgen_prior_log_sigma: np.ndarray,
    alpha_prior: tuple[float, float] = (1.0, 1.0),
    use_per_bin_sigma: bool = True,
    sigma_smooth_prior: float = 1.0,
) -> pm.Model:
    """Empirical-Bayes calibration-cohort variant of the H2.1 mixture.

    Parameters
    ----------
    y : (N_BINS,) int array
        Observed multinomial counts per 5-year bin (N_BINS = 80).
    p_conv_empirical : (N_BINS,) float
        Stage 1 empirical convention basis. Treated as fixed data.
    pgen_prior_log_mean : (N_BINS,) float
        Stage 2 reweighted log-density centring of p_gen.
    pgen_prior_log_sigma : (N_BINS,) float
        Per-bin bootstrap sigma_prior on log p_gen (optionally
        inflated by a robustness factor; see §4.2).
    alpha_prior : (a, b)
        Beta(a, b) hyperparameters for α. Default (1, 1).
    use_per_bin_sigma : bool
        If False, collapse pgen_prior_log_sigma to its median scalar.
    sigma_smooth_prior : float
        HalfNormal scale for the GRW smoothness layer. Default 1.0.
    """
    n_total = int(y.sum())
    n_bins = int(y.size)
    assert p_conv_empirical.shape == (n_bins,)
    assert pgen_prior_log_mean.shape == (n_bins,)
    assert pgen_prior_log_sigma.shape == (n_bins,)

    sigma_prior_vec = (
        pgen_prior_log_sigma
        if use_per_bin_sigma
        else np.full(n_bins, float(np.median(pgen_prior_log_sigma)))
    )

    with pm.Model() as model:
        # ── p_conv: fixed empirical vector (data input). ─────────────
        p_conv = pm.Data("p_conv_empirical", p_conv_empirical)

        # ── α: unencumbered Beta(1, 1) by default. ────────────────────
        alpha = pm.Beta(
            "alpha", alpha=alpha_prior[0], beta=alpha_prior[1],
        )

        # ── GRW smoothness scale. ────────────────────────────────────
        sigma_smooth = pm.HalfNormal(
            "sigma_smooth", sigma=sigma_smooth_prior,
        )

        # ── Non-centred GRW increments (validated by F3). ────────────
        z_pgen = pm.Normal(
            "z_pgen", mu=0.0, sigma=1.0, shape=n_bins - 1,
        )
        log_pgen_increments = pm.Deterministic(
            "log_pgen_increments", sigma_smooth * z_pgen,
        )
        # Cumsum gives the GRW path; we use it as a smoothness deviation
        # from the empirical centring rather than as the absolute shape.
        log_pgen_walk = pt.concatenate(
            [pt.zeros((1,)), pt.cumsum(log_pgen_increments)]
        )
        # Centre the GRW path on zero (otherwise the level is degenerate
        # with the softmax-additive constant) and add the empirical
        # log-density centring as a fixed offset.
        log_pgen_walk_centred = log_pgen_walk - pt.mean(log_pgen_walk)
        log_pgen = pm.Deterministic(
            "log_pgen",
            pm.math.constant(pgen_prior_log_mean)
            + sigma_prior_vec * log_pgen_walk_centred,
        )

        # Softmax to a probability vector summing to 1.
        log_pgen_shifted = log_pgen - pt.max(log_pgen)
        unnorm = pt.exp(log_pgen_shifted)
        p_gen = pm.Deterministic("p_gen", unnorm / pt.sum(unnorm))

        # ── Mixture and observation. ─────────────────────────────────
        p_mix = alpha * p_conv + (1.0 - alpha) * p_gen
        pm.Multinomial("y_obs", n=n_total, p=p_mix, observed=y)

    return model
```

Note on the construction: the empirical prior centres the *log*
densities at the cohort-derived shape; the GRW path is then used to
modulate that centring with a smoothness penalty whose magnitude is
controlled by `sigma_prior_vec`. Under a very large `sigma_prior_vec`
the empirical centring loses force and the prior reduces to a
GRW-only formulation comparable to the current model — this is the
"non-informative limit" used in the numerical-equivalence check at
§7.4. Under the recommended `sigma_prior_vec` ≈ 0.044, the centring
dominates and the GRW only modulates locally.

### 5.3 Fit invocation

```python
# === ILLUSTRATIVE — not working code ===
import pymc as pm


def fit_replicate_empirical_bayes(
    y, priors, *,
    n_draws=2_000, n_tune=1_000, n_chains=4,
    target_accept=0.95, cores=1, random_seed=42,
):
    """Fit one replicate under the empirical-Bayes model.

    `priors` is the dict returned by load_empirical_priors().
    Default sampler settings match the current production model.
    """
    model = build_model_empirical_bayes(
        y=y,
        p_conv_empirical=priors["p_conv_empirical"],
        pgen_prior_log_mean=priors["pgen_prior_log_mean"],
        pgen_prior_log_sigma=priors["pgen_prior_log_sigma"],
        alpha_prior=(1.0, 1.0),
        use_per_bin_sigma=True,
        sigma_smooth_prior=1.0,
    )
    with model:
        idata = pm.sample(
            draws=n_draws,
            tune=n_tune,
            chains=n_chains,
            cores=cores,
            random_seed=random_seed,
            progressbar=False,
            target_accept=target_accept,
            return_inferencedata=True,
            # init="auto" -> jitter+adapt_diag (default).
        )
    return idata
```

## 7. Validation tests for Stage 3 (run before the Stage 4 recovery grid)

Four tests, each cheap, each non-blocking on Martin's reply. All
four should pass before Stage 4 begins.

### 7.1 Prior-equivalence check

Sample 1 000 draws from the prior alone (no likelihood). Compute the
mean and the 5th/95th percentiles of `p_gen` across the draws, per
bin. The mean trace should match the Stage 2 `p_gen_mean` column to
within Monte-Carlo error (~ 3 % SD); the 5th/95th percentile band
should be substantially tighter than Stage 2's `p_gen_q05` /
`p_gen_q95` band (because the per-bin sigma_prior is, by Stage 2 §9,
a lower bound on prior uncertainty). Gates:

- `‖prior_mean(p_gen) − pgen_prior_mean‖_1` < 0.02 (i.e., ~ 1 % of
  total mass mis-allocated by the sampler relative to the empirical
  centring).
- `var(prior_draws(α))` ≈ 1/12 ≈ 0.083 (the Beta(1, 1) marginal
  variance) — confirms `α` is genuinely unencumbered.
- `mean(prior_draws(α))` ≈ 0.5 (Beta(1, 1) mean) — no inadvertent
  centring leak from the p_gen prior into the α prior via the
  softmax interaction.

Compute budget: ~ 30 s on sapphire (no likelihood evaluation, no
multinomial sampling).

### 7.2 Smoke test on one synthetic cell

Pick `shape=smooth_decline, alpha=0.50, tier=uniform, N=10000,
replicate=0` from the current recovery-grid synthetic data — a
mid-range cell that the current model already recovers cleanly. Fit
the modified model under defaults (Beta(1, 1), per-bin sigma_prior,
sigma_inflation = 1.5, default NUTS). Check:

- Convergence: max R-hat ≤ 1.01, min ESS-bulk ≥ 400, divergences = 0.
- α recovery: posterior mean within ± 0.05 of 0.50, 95 % CI contains 0.50.
- p_gen shape: Pearson r against true `p_gen_true` ≥ 0.95, W-1 ≤ 18.6
  years.

Compute budget: ~ 2 min on sapphire (single replicate, default
sampler).

A pass at 7.2 does not mean the model is recovery-grid-passing — it
only confirms the new code path fits cleanly without divergences. A
fail would be a hard blocker.

### 7.3 Comparison to non-centred-GRW baseline on a known-truth cell

Same cell as 7.2 but at `alpha=0.95` (where the current model is
known to be biased). Fit both:

- The non-centred-only baseline (current production model + F3's
  non-centred reparameterisation), using the existing
  `02-mixture-fit-noncentred.py`.
- The modified empirical-Bayes model.

Compare posterior α. Expected behaviour:

- Non-centred baseline: α posterior mean ≈ 0.84-0.86 (per F3's
  results at the three α=0.95 cells).
- Modified model: α posterior mean ≥ 0.90 (closer to truth), with
  the 95 % CI containing 0.95. This is the *intended* benefit of the
  empirical prior on p_gen — by anchoring p_gen externally, the
  likelihood ridge that traded α for p_gen complexity is no longer
  there to pull α toward the middle.

If the modified model produces α ≈ 0.84-0.86 in this test (i.e., no
movement vs the baseline), the empirical prior is not breaking the
ridge as intended — that's the Spektor & Kellen 2018 failure mode
(prior relocates posterior mass without resolving the ridge), and
Stage 3 needs reconsideration before committing to Stage 4.

Compute budget: 2 × 2 min ≈ 4 min on sapphire.

### 7.4 Numerical equivalence under non-informative sigma_prior

Run the smoke-test cell (7.2) again with `sigma_prior_vec` replaced
by a very large scalar (e.g., 100.0, swamping the empirical
centring). Under this setting the prior on log p_gen reduces to
something close to the current model's prior (a zero-mean GRW, with
the empirical centring effectively switched off because the
multiplicative `sigma_prior_vec` term scales the GRW path very
large relative to any centring offset). The fitted α and p_gen
should match the non-centred-only baseline's results on the same
cell to within Monte-Carlo error.

Gates:

- α posterior mean from modified model under `sigma_prior_scalar=100.0`
  agrees with non-centred baseline within ± 0.02.
- p_gen W-1 between the two models ≤ 2 years.

This test verifies that the new code path reduces to the old one as
the empirical prior is switched off — a structural sanity check that
catches algebraic errors in the parameterisation.

Compute budget: ~ 2 min on sapphire.

Total Stage 3 validation budget: ≈ 10 min wall on sapphire if run
sequentially; ≈ 4 min if 7.2 / 7.3 / 7.4 are run in parallel.

## 8. Stage 4 recovery diagnostic

The Stage 4 diagnostic re-uses the design and tooling of Experiment A:
3 cells at α = 0.95 (one each of `bimodal`, `regnal_cluster`,
`smooth_decline`) × 3 effort levels (baseline / harder / hardest) ×
1 replicate per cell-effort. Identical to F1 and F3's design; uses
the same synthetic data from
`runs/2026-05-22-recovery-grid-validation/data/synthetic-cells/`. The
plug-in change is to swap the `fit_replicate` import from
`02-cell-mixture-fit.py` to `02-mixture-fit-empirical-bayes.py`. No
new synthetic data is needed for the diagnostic tier.

Decision rule for Stage 4:

- **Pass** (proceed to full 450-cell recovery grid): all 3 α=0.95
  cells show α posterior mean within ± 0.05 of 0.95, 95 % CI
  contains truth in at least 2 of 3 cells, no divergences at
  hardest effort, R-hat ≤ 1.01.
- **Marginal pass** (proceed to a 10-replicate × 3-cell verification
  before the full grid): α posterior mean within ± 0.05 in 2 of 3
  cells; one cell with α posterior mean in (0.85, 0.90).
- **Fail** (re-consult Martin): α posterior mean ≤ 0.85 in any cell,
  or new divergences appear, or any R-hat > 1.05.

A full 450-cell recovery grid under the modified model is the same
~ 30 hours sapphire compute as the original — only triggered after a
Stage 4 diagnostic pass and Martin's confirmation of the design
decisions in §9.

## 12. Risks and contingencies

The four highest-priority failure modes, in order of severity, with
the mitigation we'd take in each case.

**Risk 1: prior-equivalence check (§7.1) fails — sampler-drawn prior
mean does not match the Stage 2 empirical mean.** Most likely cause:
an algebraic error in the centring formula (e.g., the GRW
modulation overwhelms the empirical centring at the default
`sigma_smooth ~ HalfNormal(1)` because the path can have
arbitrarily large amplitude). Mitigation: re-examine the
parameterisation; in particular, tighten `sigma_smooth_prior` from
`HalfNormal(1)` to `HalfNormal(0.2)` so the GRW path can't dominate
the centring; re-run §7.1. If still failing, fall back to a fixed
`σ_smooth = 0` (pure empirical centring + softmax, no GRW
modulation) as a Stage 3a stop-gap. Document the diagnostic so
Martin sees that the centring-vs-GRW interaction was non-trivial.

**Risk 2: smoke test (§7.2) diverges.** Most likely causes: (i) the
combination of fixed p_conv and informative p_gen prior overconstrains
the model so that the multinomial likelihood is no longer mathematically
satisfiable in the relevant region of synthetic data (which itself was
generated under a different convention basis); (ii) the
`p_conv_empirical` values are zero or near-zero in some bins and
combine with the softmax-derived p_gen to produce log-likelihood
gradients that NUTS can't follow. Mitigation: (i) check the sapphire
debug log for the specific failure; (ii) regularise `p_conv_empirical`
by adding a tiny floor (e.g., `np.maximum(p_conv_empirical, 1e-6)`)
and re-normalising; (iii) if the failure is specific to the
mismatched-basis stress test, regenerate Stage 4 synthetic data with
the empirical basis (design decision 7, alt) and rerun.

**Risk 3: comparison to baseline (§7.3) shows no improvement in α
recovery.** This is the Spektor & Kellen 2018 failure mode: the
empirical prior relocates the posterior mass without resolving the
ridge. Mitigation: this is a hard signal that the empirical p_gen
prior is not strong enough to break the ridge — either because the
ridge is genuinely deeper than the prior's information content, or
because the cohort itself is biased in a way that aligns with the
ridge (so the prior is pulling p_gen in the same direction the
likelihood ridge would have pulled it). Diagnostic moves: (i) try
`sigma_inflation = 0.5` (sharpen the prior); (ii) try the Dirichlet-
prior variant on p_conv with η = 1 000 (which can also tighten p_conv
beyond the fixed value when the empirical weights have moderate
uncertainty); (iii) escalate to Martin for the structural alternatives
he flagged in the consultation pack (ordered-mixture parameterisation,
Dirichlet process on p_gen). If §7.3 fails, **do not proceed to Stage
4 unchanged** — Stage 4 will reproduce the failure with more compute.

**Risk 4: Stage 4 passes the three α=0.95 cells but the full 450-cell
recovery grid shows residual bias at other α values.** Most likely
mechanism: the empirical prior is *over-constraining* p_gen at α
values where the true ancient activity pattern genuinely deviates
from the calibration cohort's shape. Mitigation: report the residual
bias profile (α-binned, as in F0); if the worst residual bias is
≤ ± 0.03 across all α, this is acceptable (well below the original
± 0.07) and the model is usable with a documented caveat;
if larger, reconvene with Martin on whether to further widen
`sigma_prior` or stratify the prior by α-regime.

A fifth, more diffuse risk worth flagging: **the calibration cohort
is itself a non-random subsample of LIRE** (heavily over-representing
honorific, identification, military-diploma, and milestone
inscriptions; under-representing epitaphs). The Stage 2 type-
reweighting corrects the type proportions to corpus composition, but
**within-type temporal biases persist** — e.g., if narrow-dated
epitaphs are systematically late-imperial relative to the corpus
epitaph population, the reweighted prior will inherit that
late-imperial skew. This is the residual that nothing in Stage 3 can
clean up; it has to be addressed by stratification at the per-province
or per-type level if it turns out to matter, which is outside the
Stage 3 scope. Document prominently in the Stage 4 report.
