## Category A — methodology candidates (the "what kind of model do we want?" decisions)

| ID | Approach | Pros | Cons | Cost (relative) | When to pursue |
|----|----------|------|------|----------------|----------------|
| **A-discard** | Keep only narrow-dated inscriptions (≤ 25 y or ≤ 50 y range); do SPA / scaling / event tests on this subset only | Simple, transparent, robust; no mixture-model bias issue; tens of thousands of inscriptions remain — still statistically powerful; established practice in epigraphy | Throws away 70–80 % of corpus; narrow-dated subset is itself probably biased toward military / imperial / dedicatory categories; can't say anything about α (the editorial-share quantity) | Low | Always a viable fallback; cleanest path for substantive historical questions if mixture-model fix proves expensive |
| **A-mixture** | Keep the current mixture-model approach (with documented bias caveat) | Uses all data; models the editorial process explicitly; α itself is an interesting historical-process quantity | Has the systematic α-shrinkage bias we just documented (~ 0.06 by α = 0.70); needs fix-and-revalidate before Phase 2 | Medium (re-run grid under any fix) | Default if structural fix lands well |
| **A-calibration-cohort** ⭐ | Hybrid: use the narrow-dated subset's empirical SPA as an informative prior on `p_gen` in the mixture model fitted to the full corpus | Breaks the identifiability ridge from outside; uses all data; tells us α; methodologically defensible and (per prior-art scout) genuinely novel in epigraphy | Calibration subset's biases (type / region) propagate to the prior — needs honest characterisation; implementation work to integrate the informative prior; the documented failure mode in Spektor & Kellen 2018 is that empirical priors in weakly-identified models can shift posterior location without resolving the ridge | Medium | Leading structural-fix candidate for Martin tomorrow |
| **A-hmm** | Hidden Markov Model alternative (Martin's prior suggestion) | Different framework — potentially different bias profile; Martin's home territory (state-space); well-tooled in frequentist software (Baum-Welch, Viterbi); no PyMC dependency for primary fit | Markov assumption may be too restrictive (long-memory ancient activity); convention component still needs modelling; aoristic spreading interacts awkwardly with HMM; identifiability still needs checking; less prior literature for the specific use case | High | Post-Martin if A-calibration-cohort fails or if Martin prefers HMM framing |
| **A-rigid-prior** | Strengthen the smoothness prior on p_gen (e.g., tighter `σ` on the GRW) so the genuine component can't curve enough to swap with the convention | Same identifiability-ridge intuition as A-calibration-cohort but without needing a calibration subset; cheap to test on existing grid cells | May force genuine shapes that aren't actually that smooth (assumption-testing question); known failure mode if true ancient activity has sharp transitions | Low | Quick diagnostic test pre-Martin; could be F1' (next iteration of the prior-strengthening line of investigation) |

⭐ = leading candidate as of 2026-05-24.

| **B1-a profile-likelihood plot** | Fix α at a grid of values from 0 to 1; for each, find the best-fit p_gen (MLE or posterior mode); plot the maximised log-likelihood as a function of α. ### B2. Post-Martin (when his guidance lands)

| ID | Test | Why it'd help | Cost |
|----|------|---------------|------|
| **B2-d sensitivity-to-N** | Re-fit the 3 α = 0.95 cells at very large-N (N = 200,000 or N = 500,000 synthetic, larger than any cell in the grid). | If the bias shrinks with N, the problem is identification-rate-limited (cheap fix: more data). If it persists, the problem is structural. Frequentist "consistency" check. | ~ 2 h compute. Diminishing return given F1 + F3 already strongly suggest structural. |
| **B2-e sensitivity-to-smoothness-prior** | Re-fit the 3 α = 0.95 cells under tighter / looser GRW smoothness priors on p_gen. Tighter prior → less flexible p_gen → ridge potentially broken. | Tests one specific structural fix candidate (more rigid p_gen) before recommending it broadly. Closely related to A-rigid-prior. | ~ 1 h compute |
| **B2-f Fisher-information / identifiability check** | At the data-generating parameters, numerically compute the Fisher information matrix; check its determinant / condition number. | Definitive structural-identifiability evidence in a form a frequentist statistician will accept directly. | ~ 1–2 h. Requires careful coding. |
| **B2-g multi-replicate verification** | Re-fit 10 replicates of one α = 0.95 cell at hardest settings. | Confirms the bias is replicate-robust, not a rep-000 artefact. The original investigation report flagged this as an open Limitation. | ~ 30–60 min compute |
